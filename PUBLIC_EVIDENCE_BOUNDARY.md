# Public evidence boundary

Systemic Resilience Copilot is designed to be useful with public-safe inputs without pretending that public disclosure is equivalent to supervisory information.

## What public evidence can support

Depending on what is explicitly disclosed, a public workflow can support:

- named provider or service relationships;
- the critical workload or function being discussed;
- stated failure-domain or recovery-path information;
- disclosed recovery-test results and observed recovery time;
- public incident facts and mechanism evidence;
- a reproducible evidence trail showing exactly which facts were used.

These inputs are enough to screen the recovery chain and, when evidence is explicit, diagnose some upstream states such as path independence or execution failure.

## What usually requires richer supervisory / private evidence

The public-safe product does not assume access to:

- complete bank-specific ICT architecture and identity dependencies;
- contractual recovery entitlements and priority rights;
- assured shared-recovery capacity;
- the full correlated / simultaneous demand set;
- private recovery-test evidence that has not been disclosed;
- institution-specific breaker thresholds and headroom.

Those fields matter especially when the diagnostic moves downstream toward shared demand, capacity / coordination, clean recovery and financial transmission.

## Product rule

Missing evidence is not converted into a risk conclusion.

**If a required fact is unavailable, the state remains `UNKNOWN` and the output becomes an evidence request or test.**

Therefore the public-safe demo may identify where the next supervisory question sits, but it does not claim to reproduce a confidential supervisory register or a complete census of system-level recoverability.

## What the product will not infer from public silence

- no private recovery capability does **not** follow from missing public disclosure;
- a capacity shortage does **not** follow from a shared recovery resource;
- a systemic event probability is **not** estimated;
- provider exposure is **not** converted into a provider-danger score.

## Why this limitation is useful

The architecture is intentionally information-tier aware. With richer supervisory data, the same ordered rules can populate more downstream states. With only public-safe data, the engine stops where the evidence stops and makes that boundary visible rather than filling it with assumptions.
