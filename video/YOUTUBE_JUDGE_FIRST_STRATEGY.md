# Systemic Resilience Copilot — Judge-First YouTube Strategy

## Core principle

The strongest submission video should feel like a **short product film**, not a screen recording with commentary and not a research presentation.

The video is designed for a hackathon judge who may give it only a few minutes. Every scene must prove one product capability or one decision transition.

## What the judge must understand without effort

1. A backup can exist and still fail to recover the critical workload in time.
2. Apertus turns messy natural-language recovery evidence into structured facts.
3. Deterministic rules—not the model—set the recovery state and next action.
4. Missing evidence stays `UNKNOWN`.
5. Fixing one weakness exposes the next unresolved condition instead of producing a fake “all clear”.
6. The systemic lens refuses to infer a capacity shortage from shared recovery exposure alone.

## Narrative spine

Use one memorable causal path:

**Problem → Apertus extraction → deterministic decision → Apertus explanation → narrow fix → next UNKNOWN → systemic lens → close**

Canonical proof path:

**95 min > 60 min → S5 RED / B2 → REPAIR_EXECUTION → 45 min → S6 UNKNOWN → S7 UNKNOWN**

## Directing rules

- Hook the problem in the **first 10 seconds**.
- Use the live product as the main visual asset.
- Change the visual focus every **10–20 seconds**.
- Pause on the proof beats: `95 > 60`, `S5 RED / B2`, `S6 UNKNOWN`, `S7 UNKNOWN`.
- Voice-over explains the visible action; it does not lecture about background research.
- Use clean cuts, subtle zooms and short callouts only when they help comprehension.
- No talking-head unless it adds clear value.
- No stock footage, research tables, manuscripts, equations, code tours or decorative cinematic effects.
- No generic “AI magic” language.
- Every visual effect must help the judge understand the decision path.

## Editing rhythm

Preferred total length: **about 2:20–2:40**.

Suggested beats:

- `0:00–0:12` — Hook: backup exists ≠ executable recovery
- `0:12–0:27` — Product: Apertus + transparent rules
- `0:27–0:55` — Apertus structures the synthetic scenario
- `0:55–1:18` — 95 > 60 → S5 RED / B2
- `1:18–1:37` — Apertus explains/challenges the fixed decision
- `1:37–1:57` — Apply the narrow fix → 45 min → S6 UNKNOWN
- `1:57–2:23` — Systemic lens → S7 UNKNOWN
- `2:23–2:35` — Close: Evidence → Decision → Action

## Voice principle

- confident male voice;
- natural international English;
- calm, not theatrical;
- roughly 135–150 words/minute;
- pause after the important proof lines;
- voice first, then edit product clips to the locked narration.

## Maximum major overlays

Keep no more than six strong overlays in the whole video:

1. **Backup exists ≠ executable recovery**
2. **Apertus structures the evidence**
3. **95 min > 60 min → S5 RED / B2**
4. **AI explains. Rules decide.**
5. **95 → 45 → next state becomes UNKNOWN**
6. **Shared recovery resource ≠ proven shortage**

## YouTube packaging principle

YouTube is primarily the public player for Devpost, so optimize for **judge clarity**, not clickbait.

Recommended title:

**A Backup Exists. Will It Recover in Time? | Systemic Resilience Copilot**

Recommended thumbnail:

- dark navy product frame;
- very little text;
- main visual contrast: **95 min > 60 min**;
- one small product-name mark;
- no collage of many UI panels;
- no generic AI robot imagery;
- no “INSANE / REVOLUTIONARY / GAME-CHANGER” language.

## Final acceptance rule

A judge watching only the video, without reading the paper or README, should be able to answer:

- What problem does the product solve?
- What does Apertus actually do?
- What do the deterministic rules do?
- Why is `UNKNOWN` valuable?
- What changes after the intervention?
- Where does the systemic value appear?

If any of those answers are unclear, the video is not finished.
